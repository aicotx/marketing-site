Function × level. The prescription is always the next rung, never the top.

A company that has barely sanctioned its first chatbot buys a story about autonomous agent swarms, tries to skip every step in between, and quietly writes the whole category off eighteen months later. The failure is rarely the technology. It is the jump.

There is a ladder from “a few people quietly use ChatGPT” to “processes that run themselves, supervised by exception.” We place your company on it, function by function — and we sell exactly one rung of climb at a time: scoped, priced, and proven working in your environment before anyone talks about the next.

The Ladder

Read the full methodology →

Six levels. Every company is on one of them.

“AI transformation” is a category, not a plan. A maturity ladder converts it into a diagnosis and a prescription: here is your level, per function, and here is the one move that gets you up a rung. Real organizations are lumpy — engineering at Level 2 while operations sits at Level 0 is normal, which is why we assess per function, never per company.

The audit deliverable is a heatmap like the one above: every function, its level, and the single highest-leverage climb — priced as a specific engagement, not a vision statement.

L0Shadow AI Some people use chatgpt.com. Nobody sanctioned it. L1Sanctioned chat Everyone has a license — and the license is treated as the finish line. L2Assisted workflows Real gains, all personal. The workflow leaves when its owner does. L3Standardized capability Vetted tooling, versioned and distributed. AI becomes an asset of the company. L4Autonomous operations Agents do whole tasks unattended, gated where it counts. L5Orchestrated Fleets of agents own processes end to end. Humans govern by exception.

03

Fractional

The engagement →

A senior AI engineer on your team — a few days a month.

Most companies on the lower rungs of the ladder don’t have forty hours a week of AI work. They have a steady stream of it — a workflow to build, a vendor pitch to judge, an agent misbehaving, a team that keeps asking the same good questions — and the engineers capable of handling all of that are nearly impossible to hire, because everyone else is trying to hire them too.

A fractional AI engineer is the answer to both problems: a fixed number of days each month, embedded with your team, in your environment, under your priorities. The scope rides the same roadmap the audit produced — nothing freelance about it — and it is how the ladder keeps moving between engagements rather than only during them.

The days go to

  • Building workflows from your roadmap, end to end
  • Stewarding your plugin catalog — reviews, updates, provenance
  • Keeping evaluations green as models and workflows change
  • Supervising deployed agents and tightening their trust gates
  • Office hours: your team’s questions, answered by someone who ships

Fixed days per month · three-month minimum · pairs with any level

How we work

Our approach →

One rung at a time.

A Level-1 company asking for agent swarms gets told no, and why, with the ladder as the explanation. Everyone else is selling the teleporter; declining to is the most valuable thing we do.

Evidence over demos.

Every build ships with an evaluation harness. If we can’t measure that the workflow works — on your data, against your definition of “works” — we don’t call it done.

Your environment, your data.

Work is deployed inside your infrastructure and wired to your systems. Nothing valuable about how your company operates should live on a consultant’s laptop.

We run on what we sell.

The firm’s own operations run at Levels 4 and 5: long-running agents triage our mail, watch our infrastructure, and ship our software daily. When we demo autonomous operations, we demo our own.
